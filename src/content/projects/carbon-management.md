## The challenge

Measuring a company's carbon footprint is one thing. Understanding how to reduce it, tracking progress, and identifying where to focus efforts is another challenge entirely.

At CO2 AI, I joined the **Measure team**, which worked on turning raw emissions data into meaningful insights that companies could use to understand their environmental impact and plan their reduction strategies.

What made this experience particularly interesting was the combination of a complex business domain and a modern, data-intensive technology stack.

My role was to contribute to product capabilities that made carbon data more actionable, while also helping the team maintain a clean and sustainable codebase as the product evolved.

## Building features that connect data to decisions

As a Senior Software Engineer, I worked on two key areas of the product: **Targets** and **Strategic Levers**.

Both addressed an important question: once a company understands its carbon footprint, how can it use that information to make progress?

### Targets — Tracking carbon-reduction goals

I designed and developed the Targets module, which helped clients track their carbon-footprint reduction objectives in detail.

The challenge was to translate emissions data and reduction goals into functionality that users could understand and work with.

This meant thinking beyond the implementation itself and considering how the underlying data, business rules, and user experience fit together.

I focused on building functionality that could support the product's needs while remaining understandable and maintainable for the engineering team.

### Strategic Levers — Supporting reduction strategies

I also contributed to Strategic Levers, a module designed to provide tailored strategic recommendations based on different emissions categories.

The idea was to help companies move from understanding their emissions to exploring potential ways of reducing them.

Working on this module required engaging with the business domain and translating its concepts into product functionality.

What I found especially interesting was the connection between engineering decisions and the way users could interpret and act on complex environmental data.

## Working across the stack

The product combined **React on the frontend and Python on the backend**, with PostgreSQL, RabbitMQ, and Docker as part of the technical environment.

Coming from a background strongly rooted in C# and .NET architecture, this was an opportunity to apply the same engineering principles in a different ecosystem.

The technologies were different, but the questions remained familiar:

- How do we keep responsibilities clear as features grow?
- How do we organize code so that other developers can understand and extend it?
- How do we balance delivering features with maintaining a healthy codebase?
- How do we make technical decisions that support the product rather than complicate it?

I approached the work with those principles in mind, combining hands-on feature development with attention to code structure and maintainability.

## Improving the way we worked

Beyond developing product features, I contributed to improving the team's engineering practices.

Part of that work involved **helping establish development standards and reorganizing the repository around clean-code principles**.

The goal wasn't to impose a particular coding style or introduce unnecessary rules. It was to make the codebase more consistent, easier to navigate, and simpler to maintain as new functionality was introduced.

I believe a well-organized repository and clear development conventions make a real difference in everyday engineering work, especially when multiple developers are contributing to the same product.

These improvements were an important part of my contribution alongside feature delivery.

## Sharing knowledge and supporting the team

I also had the opportunity to mentor junior developers.

For me, mentoring isn't just about reviewing code or explaining how a particular technology works. It's about helping someone understand the reasoning behind technical decisions and become more confident in making their own.

I shared knowledge, provided technical guidance, and supported developers as they worked through implementation challenges.

I see this as a natural part of being a senior engineer: **contributing through the code you write, but also through the people you help grow**.

## Why it mattered

During my time at CO2 AI, I contributed to both the product and the engineering practices supporting its development.

My main contributions included:

- Designing and developing the **Targets** module for detailed carbon-reduction tracking.
- Contributing to **Strategic Levers**, supporting emissions-reduction recommendations by category.
- Working across a React and Python application backed by PostgreSQL and RabbitMQ.
- Helping establish shared development standards and improve repository organization.
- Mentoring junior developers and sharing engineering knowledge.

These contributions brought together product development, codebase maintainability, and team collaboration within a domain where data and business logic play a central role.

## What I took away

This experience reinforced something I've come to appreciate throughout my career: **strong engineering principles aren't tied to a particular language or framework**.

Whether I'm working with .NET and WPF or React and Python, the fundamentals remain the same: understand the problem, keep the design clear, manage complexity, and build software that others can confidently evolve.

It also reminded me how rewarding it can be to work on products where complex data needs to become something genuinely useful to the people making decisions.

For me, CO2 AI was an opportunity to combine hands-on product development, engineering discipline, and knowledge sharing in a new technical and business environment.
